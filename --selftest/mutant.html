How to use it

  1. Photograph each page. Press the big button and take one photo per page, in the order they are printed. Flat on a table, in good light — the answers are only as good as the small print.
  2. Or pick them out of your gallery. The button on the left opens it, and the number on it is how many pages you have so far. Tap a page to take it out or move it.
  3. The camera stays open. Tap the shutter and it takes the page — no “use photo / retake”, no leaving the app. Keep going, page after page, and press Done when the paper is finished. (Where a browser will not give the page a camera of its own, the 📷 button opens your phone's camera app instead, exactly as it always did.)
  4. Press ✓ when the paper is complete. Every question printed on the pages comes back answered — multiple choice, fill in the blank, open-ended and lettered parts alike.

A question that carries on over the page is fine: the pages are read as one paper, so it is stitched back together instead of coming back as two halves.

Say what you want, in writing or out loud

The box above the three buttons takes a typed question, and the 🎤 beside it takes a spoken one where your browser can hear you. Say a Chinese question in Chinese and set the subject to Chinese — the dictation listens in the language of the paper.

  • With photos — what you ask governs the run. “Only question 5” does question 5 and leaves the rest out; “check my working on the last page” checks that; “P5 maths, test on Friday” is taken as background and every question is still done.
  • With no photo at all — press ✓ with just the box filled in and it simply answers what you asked. Ask for five practice questions and five come back, one card each.

It marks what is already written

You do not have to choose. Every question is looked at for an answer already written on the paper: where there is one it is marked — right, partly right or wrong — with a line or two of feedback saying exactly where it went wrong and what to do instead, and the correct answer beside it. Where the question is still blank it is simply answered. A page holding some of each comes back with some of each.

The correct answer is always worked out from scratch first, so what the student wrote can never talk the app into the wrong answer.

📋 And then the report on the whole paper

Once every page has been read, the marked script is looked at as a whole and the report appears above the answers: the score, then what the student can already do, what their mistakes have in common — the same slip in questions 3, 7 and 12 is one misunderstanding, not three — and what to go and practise. It writes itself; there is nothing to switch on.

The score is counted here, not by the AI, from the marks already on the cards: out of the paper's own marks where every question prints one, and otherwise a question at a time with a partly right answer worth half. A question you left blank is never counted against you, and an answer the AI would not commit to a verdict on is left out rather than marked wrong.

A paper with nothing written on it gets no report — there is nothing to report on, only answers. The report goes out with Copy and Print; press 📋 Report to have it written again.

📕 Your mistake book

Every question you get wrong or partly right on a scanned paper is kept in your own mistake book — the question, its diagram, what you wrote, the right answer and the feedback. It fills itself as you scan; the number on 📕 My questions at the top of the screen is how many you have kept, and every card has a 📕 chip that takes it back out again in one tap.

This is the only thing this app keeps. Your photographs are not saved, your right answers are not saved, and nobody else can see your book — it is under your own account, and your teacher's book is their own.

Questions take themselves out again. Print the worksheet, do it, and then photograph it back in like any other paper. Every question you get fully right counts once — get the same one right twice in a row and it leaves your mistake book for good. Get it wrong in between and the count goes back to the start, because two in a row is the whole point: once is luck often enough to matter. A question you leave blank changes nothing either way. The card says where each one is up to (right 1 of 2), and so does the book.

📗 Your learning list

Some questions are worth doing again even though you got them right — the ones you were unsure about, or the kind that keeps coming up. On any question you have scanned, right, wrong or not attempted, press 📗 Add to learning list and it is kept alongside your mistake book, on its own tab.

It works the same way in every respect but one: nothing leaves it by itself. Your mistake book empties as you get questions right, because that is what it is for; the learning list is what you chose to keep, so it stays until you take it out. Worksheets are made from it in exactly the same way.

💬 Ask Mr Chung

Stuck on one? Every question in either list has a 💬 Ask Mr Chung button. It takes the picture of the question this app already cut out of your photograph, cleans the page up to plain black and white, and opens WhatsApp with a message to him already written — who you are, which question, and what it says. On a phone the picture goes with it; on a computer it goes as a link. You still press send yourself, and nothing is sent unless you do.

Open it, tick the questions you want to try again, and press 📤 Make a worksheet. A link is sent to the email you sign in with, and the page it opens is the questions on their own with the answers on a page at the back — ready to print. It opens cleaned up: the printing sharpened to black and white and your own working rubbed out, so the question is blank again. One switch on the page brings back the original photograph instead. The worksheet keeps for a year and only you and your teacher can open it.

If an answer is not good enough, fix it — and teach it

Every answer card carries a ✎ Edit — yours alone, never a student's. Rewrite the answer, the explanation, the mark and the feedback, and the card is yours — that is what Copy and Print hand over. Underneath there is a box for the rule behind the correction: type it and it is saved as a standing instruction in your notebook, obeyed word for word from the very next question — here, in Ans Key and in the Science Learning Portal, because all three read the same notebook. Tick Remember my answer to this question and your corrected answer is filed with it as a key fact.

Maths is answered the way it is taught — never with algebra

Every maths answer uses the methods the PSLE is marked on: the unitary method (units and parts), a model described in words, before-and-after, working backwards, the assumption method. No letters standing for unknowns and no equations to solve — an answer that gets the right number by algebra is one a pupil cannot reproduce in the exam.

Every maths answer is checked afterwards, and one that slipped into algebra is rewritten before it reaches the screen. A question that prints the algebra itself — “simplify 3x + 5x” — is answered as the paper asks. If an answer still uses algebra after that, the card says ⚠ uses algebra rather than hiding it, so it can be put right with ✎ Edit.

👣 One step at a time

Every card that has real working to show hands it over one step at a time. Press Show me the first step, read the line of working and the sentence under it saying why it is done, then press Next step — and keep going. Each step is a line a student could have written themselves, and the last one is the answer.

On a question that is still blank the answer waits behind the steps, because an answer printed above the working is the only line that gets read. On a question the student has already answered it is shown straight away — the mark is already on the card, and a script being marked must not have to be clicked through. The full Why waits with the steps on both, since it is the same solution written out in one go.

Show all working on a card opens that one; 👣 Show all working at the top of the answers opens the whole paper at once, and presses again to close it back up for a second go. Copy and Print always carry every step, whatever is on the screen at the time. Set How much explanation to Answer only on the Settings card below and there are no steps at all.

Four subjects, and every card says which

Science, Mathematics, English and Chinese. A Chinese paper is answered and marked in Chinese; an English comprehension gets an answer in full sentences, and a maths question gets the working line by line.

Every answer card wears the subject it was read as — Mathematics question, Science question — worked out from the question itself, so a mixed pile of pages says on each card which standard that question was answered and marked to. Where a question did not say, the subject on the Settings card below is used and the chip says so.

Nothing is saved anywhere. Your paper lives in this tab and leaves through Copy or Print.

📥 Keeping a question you have just read

Every answer card carries a 📥 Send to vetting button, and the header carries 📥 Send all to vetting for a whole paper. Choose the portal it belongs to — Science, Maths, English or Chinese — and the question, its options, its answer and the reason why are written straight into that app's vetting list.

Each question goes to the app for its own subject. The subject is worked out from the question itself, so a pile holding two subjects files itself correctly and a maths question never lands in the science list. The button on the card says where it is going before you press it, and you can always choose a different list. Set Subject in the settings and the whole paper goes to that one app instead. A question whose subject cannot be told is never guessed at — it stays here.

It lands in vetting, never in the bank: the card is highlighted purple there and says it came from this app, so you know to check the wording and attach the diagram before you approve it. The topic is left for you to choose — it belongs to that app's syllabus, not this one.

What the student wrote, the mark and the feedback stay here. A bank question is the question, not somebody's marked answer to it. This is a different door from ✎ Edit on a card, which fixes the answer and teaches the rule behind it.

Whose answers these are

Which AI is answering

You should not normally need this. The backup's real key lives on the server, as a Firebase secret behind the askOpenAi Cloud Function, which is what lets a student's phone use it with nothing set up on it. Set it once with firebase functions:secrets:set OPENAI_API_KEY and deploy the functions in the Maths repo.

Kimi (Moonshot AI) is a third supplier, on its own account and its own cap — so a day that takes out both of the others does not take out this app. Its server key is MOONSHOT_API_KEY behind the askKimi Cloud Function, set the same way. Moonshot renames its flagship with every release, so the model is a box, not a fixed list: 🔄 Load models asks your own account which ids it will answer to, and a stale id is the first thing to check if Kimi starts refusing every call.

A key typed here is only a fallback for this browser — useful before the function is deployed, or if it ever stops answering. It is kept in this browser, never uploaded and never written into a paper or the repository, and it is the very same key your Maths, English, Chinese and Science portals use: one already saved in any of them is already being used here.
